# 2. Fixed anchors, variable everything else

Date: 2026-08-24

## Status

Accepted

## Context

The athlete explicitly wants variety: different protocols in different weeks — a pairing week, a hard
single-group week, a to-failure week — to keep training interesting. Boredom is a real risk and
a programme that isn't followed is worth nothing, so this is a legitimate requirement rather
than a preference to be argued away.

But rotating the method weekly destroys the ability to measure. If week 1 is paired at high
density, week 2 is heavy straight sets and week 3 is taken to failure, then when week 4's
numbers come in low there is no way to distinguish accumulated fatigue from a protocol that
simply isn't comparable. Progressive overload requires something held constant to measure
against. This is the substantive objection to "muscle confusion": not that variety is harmful,
but that undifferentiated variety leaves no signal.

The influences named for this programme also disagree with each other. Dorian Yates prescribes
very low volume taken past failure; Galpin and Huberman lean on the volume literature — 10–20
hard sets per muscle per week, most sets at RIR 1–3, two exposures per muscle. Both cannot be
the skeleton.

Options considered:

- **Weekly theme rotation.** Maximum novelty, no comparable weeks.
- **One fixed protocol, exercise variety only.** Cleanest signal, but swapping exercises resets
  the load comparison anyway, and may not deliver the novelty actually wanted.
- **Autoregulated daily mode selection.** Most adaptive, messiest data, hardest to plan from.
- **Two separated layers.**

## Decision

The programme is split into two layers with different rules.

**Anchors** — back squat, barbell RDL, low-incline dumbbell press, neutral-grip pull-up. Four
lifts, one per movement pattern. They appear every week, keep the same rep scheme, and have
their load progressed on a defined weekly schedule. They are performed first in their session,
at full rest, never paired, and never taken to failure.

**The variable layer** — accessories, pairing, rep ranges, rest intervals, and where failure is
permitted. This is governed by the mesocycle **theme**, which changes every four weeks.

The volume model is the skeleton: 10–20 hard sets per muscle per week, two exposures per muscle,
most sets at RIR 1–3. Yates is borrowed narrowly — true-failure sets are permitted only on the
last isolation movement of a muscle group, where joint risk is low and recovery cost is small.

## Consequences

Variety arrives at month scale rather than week scale, and within a week it arrives through
accessories rather than through changing the whole method. This is less novelty than was
originally asked for, and that trade is deliberate.

In exchange, one question stays answerable at all times: are the anchors moving? Four numbers,
tracked continuously across every theme, deload and disruption.

Anchor selection becomes a high-cost decision, since an anchor is loaded progressively for
months. This is why joint constraints recorded in the athlete profile drive anchor choice rather
than being worked around after the fact.

Because the anchors survive theme changes, a bad mesocycle is diagnosable: if the anchors kept
moving, the theme was fine and something else was wrong; if they stalled, the theme was too
much.
